# User Prompt Guide: How to Write Better Novel Prompts

## 4. User Prompt (The Most Important Prompt!)

Your initial prompt is the **creative foundation** for the entire novel. The system prompt provides quality standards, but YOUR prompt defines the story. Here's how to write prompts that produce exceptional results:

---

## Prompt Structure Template

```
Create a [genre] novel with [X] chapters about:

**Setting:** [Time period, location, world-building details]

**Protagonists:**
- [Name]: [Age, background, personality, goal, flaw]
- [Name]: [Age, background, personality, goal, flaw]

**Antagonist(s):**
- [Name]: [Motivation, methods, relationship to protagonist]

**Plot:**
- Inciting incident: [What kicks off the story]
- Central conflict: [The main problem/question]
- Major plot points: [Key events you want to see]
- Climax: [How the conflict peaks]
- Resolution style: [Happy ending? Ambiguous? Tragic?]

**Themes:** [What the story is really about]

**Tone:** [Dark, hopeful, satirical, melancholic, etc.]

**POV:** [First person, third limited, third omniscient]

**Tense:** [Past or present]

**Special requests:** [Anything else - writing style, specific scenes, etc.]
```

---

## Example: Basic vs. Excellent Prompts

### ❌ Basic Prompt (Vague):
```
"Write a mystery novel with 10 chapters about a detective solving a murder"
```

**Problems:**
- No character details (detective could be anyone)
- No setting specifics (modern? historical? location?)
- No unique angle or hook
- No tone guidance (gritty? cozy? noir?)
- No plot specifics beyond "solving a murder"

**Result:** Generic, forgettable story with stock characters

---

### ✅ Excellent Prompt (Detailed):
```
Create a psychological mystery novel with 10 chapters:

**Setting:**
- Modern day Manhattan, primarily in a converted warehouse loft and subway system
- November, unseasonably warm, creating an unsettling atmosphere

**Protagonist:**
- Maya Rodriguez: 32, former forensic psychologist turned private detective
- Left NYPD after a case where she correctly profiled a killer but wasn't believed
- Brilliantly intuitive but struggles with impostor syndrome
- Has synesthesia - sees people's emotions as colors
- Goal: Prove she was right about the killer, restore her reputation

**Antagonist:**
- The "Conductor": Serial killer Maya profiled 3 years ago
- Creates elaborate "performances" using crime scenes
- Obsessed with Maya as his only "worthy audience"
- Leaves clues only she can decipher using her synesthesia

**Plot:**
- Inciting incident: Maya receives anonymous package with crime scene photo and "The show's not over"
- Central conflict: Conductor is killing again, taunting Maya to "solve his masterpiece"
- Major plot points:
  - Chapter 3: Maya realizes the killer knows about her synesthesia (impossible - she's never told anyone)
  - Chapter 5: A victim survives, but has amnesia
  - Chapter 7: Maya discovers the victims are connected to her past case
  - Chapter 9: Climactic confrontation in abandoned subway station
- Resolution: Maya catches killer but learns disturbing truth about her own past

**Themes:**
- Perception vs reality
- The cost of being right when no one believes you
- Whether our gifts can become our prisons

**Tone:**
- Tense psychological thriller with noir influences
- Claustrophobic despite urban setting
- Unreliable narrator elements

**POV:** First person (Maya's perspective)

**Tense:** Present tense (for immediacy)

**Special requests:**
- Each chapter should end with a cryptic message from the Conductor
- Weave in Maya's synesthesia naturally (not just a gimmick)
- Include flashbacks to the original case without being confusing
- Build romantic subplot with her former partner (now assigned to the case)
```

**Result:** Unique, compelling story with memorable characters, clear direction, and rich details for the AI to work with

## Prompt Writing Tips

### 1. **Character Depth is Everything**
- Give characters GOALS (what they want)
- Give characters FLAWS (what holds them back)
- Give characters BACKSTORY (why they are who they are)
- Show RELATIONSHIPS (dynamics create conflict)

### 2. **Specificity Creates Authenticity**
- "A city" → "1920s Chicago during Prohibition"
- "A warrior" → "A 28-year-old female samurai questioning her clan's honor code"
- "In the future" → "2157, Mars colony struggling with oxygen shortage"

### 3. **Plot Structure Clarity**
- Don't just say "adventure" - outline the journey
- Identify key turning points
- Specify the stakes (what happens if protagonist fails?)

### 4. **Tone and Atmosphere**
- Use reference points: "Tone like Gillian Flynn" or "Atmosphere of Blade Runner"
- Describe the feeling you want: "hopeful despite darkness" vs "relentlessly bleak"

### 5. **Technical Choices**
- **POV matters hugely**:
  - First person = intimate, limited knowledge
  - Third limited = close but more flexible
  - Third omniscient = multiple perspectives, epic scope
- **Tense matters**:
  - Present = immediate, urgent
  - Past = reflective, traditional

### 6. **Constraints Can Enhance Creativity**
- "The protagonist never speaks" (challenge: convey character without dialogue)
- "Each chapter from a different character's POV"
- "Story takes place in 24 hours"

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### ❌ Too Vague
```
"Write a sci-fi novel about space"
```
→ Space opera? Hard sci-fi? Space horror? Comedy? Near future? Far future?

### ❌ Conflicting Instructions
```
"Write a lighthearted comedy about a serial killer"
```
→ Pick a tone that matches your content

### ❌ Overloading Plot Points
```
"Include: alien invasion, time travel, romance, mystery, political intrigue,
space battles, coming-of-age, heist, supernatural powers, amnesia..."
```
→ Focus on 1-2 main plot threads for a cohesive story

### ❌ No Character Agency
```
"Things happen to the protagonist and they react"
```
→ Protagonists should DRIVE the plot with their choices

## Quick Reference: Genre-Specific Elements

### Mystery/Thriller
- Clearly establish the central mystery/threat
- Provide red herrings or misdirection opportunities
- Define the stakes (ticking clock?)
- Specify detective's unique approach/skill

### Romance
- What prevents the couple from being together (internal and external obstacles)?
- Character growth required for relationship to work
- Tone: sweet, steamy, angsty, slow-burn?
- HEA (happily ever after) or HFN (happy for now)?

### Fantasy
- Magic system rules and limitations
- World-building essentials (geography, cultures, conflicts)
- Protagonist's place in this world
- What makes this world unique (avoid generic medieval)

### Sci-Fi
- Technology level and its implications
- Central "what if?" question
- Balance between ideas and character
- Hard sci-fi (realistic science) vs. soft sci-fi (focus on social implications)

### Literary Fiction
- Theme is paramount (what are you exploring?)
- Character interiority and change
- Prose style matters more
- Plot can be quieter, more character-driven

## Final Thoughts

**The agent will faithfully execute YOUR vision.** The better your prompt:
- The more unique and compelling the story
- The more consistent the character voices
- The more coherent the plot
- The more authentic the world feels

Think of yourself as a film director giving detailed instructions to a talented screenwriter.
The more specific your vision, the better the result.

**Remember:** You can always refine as you go! After seeing Chapter 1, you can say:
- "Make the protagonist more cynical"
- "Add more humor to the dialogue"
- "Slow down the pacing in action scenes"
- "Develop the antagonist's motivation more"

The agent adapts to your feedback while maintaining continuity.
